Two Certifications, One Degree — What Actually Unlocks the Work

Intuit QBO Certified User (2026), CompTIA Project+, and a B.S. in Business–Marketing from WGU. Two of these run the work. One of them is why ToyBox exists at all.

Two operational credentials and one educational credential — Intuit QuickBooks Online Certified User (2026), CompTIA Project+, and a B.S. in Business–Marketing from Western Governors University. Each one carries a different piece of what ToyBox actually does. Here is what each one unlocks.

Intuit QBO Certified User

First, the frame — because the QBO cert changes what I can offer, but not what your organization needs.

Your organization needs a bookkeeper. Someone (in-house or outsourced) whose day-to-day job is coding transactions, reconciling accounts, and running the ongoing books. That is not what ToyBox is. If ongoing bookkeeping is the primary need, ToyBox is not the right hire — go find a good bookkeeper.

What the QBO certification unlocks is interim financial-operations support that sits above the bookkeeping line. Different work. Complementary to the bookkeeper you have (or should hire).

Concretely, that looks like:

  • Interim financial oversight during a transition — a departing controller, a leadership gap, a compliance retrofit — where the bookkeeper is still running the books but the organization needs a senior financial operator holding the systems.
  • Discovery-scoped cleanup or setup projects when the books need serious rework and the current bookkeeper cannot lift it alone. Scope determined in discovery, case by case.
  • QuickBooks Online configuration & discipline — chart of accounts, class structure, location structure, tagging conventions — so the reports coming out of QBO answer the questions your board and funders actually ask.
  • Financial-systems oversight at the operational level, not the ledger level. What the numbers mean, what pattern they are showing, and what needs a decision this month.

All finance-related engagements are scoped in discovery. The right shape of the work depends on where your organization is, what your bookkeeper is doing, and what is missing. I do not publish a fixed menu because the fixed menu is almost always wrong.

The QBO credential also pairs directly with the City of Seattle Management Services and Project Management roster categories. Departments needing interim financial-operations support can bring ToyBox in under those rosters.

CompTIA Project+

The Project+ certification is the discipline behind how the work gets delivered. It is the framework for scoping, sequencing, staffing, and closing a body of work with the paperwork intact — the difference between a project that runs on vibes and a project that runs on documentation.

For ToyBox this means every engagement has:

  • A scoped charter — what is in, what is out, what the deliverables are, and how we know we are done.
  • A phase plan with milestones the client can actually verify.
  • Risk logs and issue tracking — so the client always knows what is blocking, what has slipped, and what changed.
  • A close-out with the artifacts (documentation, playbooks, dashboards) handed off — so the systems live on after the engagement ends.

The one that made ToyBox possible: B.S. Business Management (Marketing Concentration), WGU

The technical certifications explain how the work is run. The marketing degree explains why ToyBox exists at all.

Here is the timeline. ToyBox launched in 2008. By 2012 — four years in — the work had already grown to include marketing, communications, and public-relations consulting for the organizations that were trusting me with the front end of their operations. Newsletters. Rebrands. Board-facing communications. Media relations. Campaign production. That work created the demand for a formal grounding, and that is the year I enrolled at Western Governors University. I finished the B.S. Business Management (Marketing Concentration) in 2018.

Operations work is invisible by design. A well-run close is invisible. A functioning compliance calendar is invisible. A board report that is finally readable is invisible. And "invisible" is a hard thing to sell — until you can name it, position it, and tell the story of what it does for the organization.

That is the marketing discipline. Not the "put a logo on a mug" version. The strategic one — audience, positioning, message architecture, brand voice, storytelling. The training that turns "we did some operations consulting" into "we built the operational infrastructure that let this organization keep its promises."

For a fuller version of how the creative and marketing side shaped the early years of ToyBox — including the social-media-marketing era before algorithms, and one mixtape promotion that pulled almost 90,000 downloads — see the Converge Media interview.

What the WGU degree unlocked for ToyBox specifically:

  • The brand itself. Naming, positioning, and holding a firm that operates at the intersection of nonprofit, government, and private-sector work — without diluting into "we do everything."
  • The frameworks language. Turning tacit expertise into named methodologies (Decision → Enforcement → Consequence, and more coming) that clients can hold onto and boards can reference.
  • Client communication. Translating operational reality into board-ready language. The reason ToyBox reports get read is that they were designed to be read.
  • Public-sector positioning. The Seattle Consultant Roster application, the WA State Commerce narratives, the roster capability statements — all of it lives at the intersection of strategy and communications. The degree is what lets ToyBox compete for those seats.
  • This blog. Twenty years of operations work only becomes a public voice when someone can write it down in a way that lands. That is not accidental. That is training.

Together, they answer a specific concern

The concern is this: "If I bring in a fractional operator, how do I know they will actually finish? How do I know the systems will still work when they leave? And how do I know they can explain what they built to the people who need to keep using it?"

Project+ answers the first question. QBO answers the second, specifically for financial systems. The marketing degree answers the third — because a system nobody understands is a system that decays the moment the consultant leaves.
