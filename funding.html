Open funding calls for King County organizations

Grant and contract opportunities for the people ToyBox works with: nonprofits, cultural organizations, small businesses and public agencies. Every listing here was opened and read on the funder's own page, not pulled from a search result.

Last checked: November 14, 2026. This list is reviewed every Friday.

Open now

Three calls you can apply to today

Deadlines are listed as the funder states them. Confirm the date on the funder's page before you spend time on an application.

  • Best Starts for Kids, King County

    Workforce Development Mini Grants, round 3

    Who can apply
    Organizations serving King County communities with 25 or fewer employees. That includes nonprofits, community-based organizations, tribes and tribal organizations, early childhood development centers, and licensed childcare centers and programs. Organizations led by Black, Indigenous and other communities of color are strongly encouraged to apply.
    Amount
    Up to $9,999 per grant, out of $300,000 for this round.
    What it pays for
    Staff training in healthy childhood development, infant and early childhood mental health, racial equity, and disability justice.
    Deadline
    2 PM on November 25, 2026
    How to apply
    Through King County DCHS Agiloft. Your organization needs an Agiloft account first, so start that step early. Look for the solicitation named "Workforce Development Mini Grants RFA Round 3."

    Checked on the Best Starts blog, November 14, 2026.

  • Washington State Department of Commerce

    WorkFirst service providers (request for proposals)

    Who can apply
    Community-based organizations that want to deliver Commerce WorkFirst services.
    Amount
    Commerce says it will award 27 contracts statewide, one per WorkFirst service area. No dollar figure is posted. The contract period runs July 1, 2027 through June 30, 2029.
    What it pays for
    Work experience programs for families receiving Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF): case management, career development, subsidized employment and related support.
    Deadline
    3 PM PT on January 14, 2027
    One thing to watch
    The posting says the proposal period opens November 17, 2026. If the application is not live yet, check back after that date.

    Checked on commerce.wa.gov, November 14, 2026.

  • Washington State Department of Commerce

    Behavioral Health Facilities closure prevention

    Who can apply
    Nonprofits, for-profit businesses, public entities, tribes and Indian health care providers, community hospitals and community-based behavioral health providers. The facility has to be licensed and operating, has to serve publicly funded clients, and you have to show it is at real risk of closing within two years without state money.
    Amount
    $9.7 million total. Awards are first come, first served until the money runs out.
    What it pays for
    Repairs and upgrades that keep an existing facility open, including work needed to meet licensing standards. It does not pay for expansion. Grant money has to be spent by June 30, 2027, and you have to keep the contracted use of the building for at least 10 years.
    Deadline
    Midnight June 30, 2027, or sooner if the funds run out

    Checked on commerce.wa.gov, November 14, 2026.

How this page works

What we check, and what we will not do

Every listing is verified on the funder's page

Nothing goes on this page from a search result or a summary. Someone opens the funder's own page and reads it. If a detail is not stated there, this page says so instead of guessing.

Verified, unverified and absent are three different things, and this page tells you which one applies. A detail confirmed on the funder's page is stated plainly. A detail that comes from somewhere else, say a funder's deadline that appears on another organization's list but not on the funder's own site, is marked confirm first and says where it came from. A detail nobody states is called not stated. You should never have to guess which kind you are reading.

Funders get things wrong too. Where a funder's own page contradicts itself, this page gives the date and names the error, because a reader planning around the wrong weekday misses the deadline.

Closed calls come off

Deadlines go stale fast. The list is reviewed every Friday. A call comes off this page as soon as its deadline passes, or as soon as the funder marks it closed. If a funder's page still says "open" but the posted deadline has passed, we treat it as closed and leave it off. Each listing carries the date it was last checked, so you can see how fresh it is.

Listing is not recommending. Wherever an opportunity appears, this page and hers tell you a call exists and what it says. Neither ranks calls, scores your odds, or tells you to apply. That decision is yours, and nothing here suggests she can help an application land.

ToyBox does not take paid work on applications to 4Culture or the Community Reinvestment Program while she holds those positions. Not at a discount, not through a partner. No funder pays for a place on this page, and ToyBox has no part in who gets funded.

Where ToyBox fits

Finding the call is the easy part

Most organizations do not lose funding because they missed the listing.

They lose it because a registration had lapsed, the budget did not match the story the narrative told, the board had never adopted a policy the funder asks about, or nobody had worked out who would track the money and file the reports once it arrived. That work happens months before a deadline, and it is the work ToyBox does.

  • Nonprofits and cultural organizations

    Grant readiness and grant compliance: registrations, financial controls, fund accounting, and the reporting you will owe after the award. Also theory of change and program evaluation, which is what most funders are really asking you to show.

  • Small businesses and firms

    Government contracting readiness: how to read a solicitation, what a scope actually needs, getting onto a roster, and the paperwork that stops otherwise good bids. Public money is not only for nonprofits.
